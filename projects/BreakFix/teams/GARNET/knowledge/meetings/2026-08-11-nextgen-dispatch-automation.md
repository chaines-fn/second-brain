## Overview

This was a Field Week working session to brainstorm the next generation of Smart Dispatch, with Unisys's unmet needs as the driving case study. The group spent most of the session dissecting why Unisys — Smart Dispatch's original co-creator buyer — still can't fully adopt it: Unisys dispatches through Salesforce Agentforce in daily batches, considering an entire territory's work orders and available crews at once, while Field Nation's model dispatches one work order at a time. The specific pain point is that Unisys wants the same technician sent to multiple work orders at the same site on the same day (about 19% of their work orders today involve this), and bundling — the obvious-sounding fix — has a bad reputation on the team because of stuck-bundle risk when no single provider can take everything in a group.

The session also revisited a recurring strategic question: should Smart Dispatch move toward AI/heuristic decisioning, or stay a deterministic rules engine? The group reaffirmed determinism, citing past friction when buyers wanted to tweak ranking (which undercut the provider-quality team's ranking model) and unresolved compliance concerns about AI-driven assignment bias. AI was framed as a possible complement (e.g., interpreting buyer language into rules, or handling escalation) rather than a replacement for the core engine. A second theme was replacing fixed dispatch time windows with continuous, "keep trying until something escalates" dispatching, modeled loosely on DoorDash's approach.

The concrete outcome: the platform will detect same-site, multi-work-order situations itself via geocoding and bundle them automatically — reversing the team's prior stance that this was a model Field Nation doesn't support. This is scoped narrowly as the *first* step in a larger geography-and-batch capability, not a standalone feature, and there's no technical design, estimate, or owner yet. Several harder problems were parked explicitly: capacity caps (the team has no way to state realistic daily throughput per provider/territory), broader "nearby but not identical site" clustering, and a data-integrity flag — a provider credited with 200 same-day check-ins at one site, suggesting bot/API abuse rather than real work.

## Key Notes

- **Same-site bundling: system-computed via geocoding, not buyer- or provider-authored.** The group decided Field Nation's own system — not Unisys, not the provider — will detect when multiple work orders share a geocoded address and bundle them automatically before publishing to the service territory. This reverses GARNET's earlier position (in the knowledge base) that bundling was a model Field Nation doesn't support. Geocoding was judged sufficient to resolve "same building, different suite" cases; H3 cells were discussed but rejected for this specific problem as unnecessary added precision/database cost given geocoding already collapses address variants to the same coordinates.

- **Why not let Unisys's Salesforce layer decide the bundle upstream instead:** One proposal (attributed to Ryan) was to have Unisys represent service territories as an abstract "crew" resource in Salesforce rather than exposing every individual provider — Agentforce would tag/bundle multi-WO groups before they ever reach Field Nation, and FN would simply receive pre-bundled work. The group leaned away from this: it would require Unisys to build a per-provider representation in their own Salesforce instance (more PII exposure, more maintenance on their end), whereas keeping the bundling logic inside Field Nation's own system was seen as more contained and elegant.

- **Unisys operates on a fundamentally different dispatch model — daily batch vs. per-work-order.** Unisys's Agentforce console looks at their full day's/territory's work orders against all available crews at once, factoring in geography and a flat travel-time buffer between sites (e.g., 30 minutes in Manhattan, up to an hour in Tucson) — not real driving-time routing, just a uniform buffer plus capacity math. Field Nation currently evaluates and dispatches work orders individually. Open research question (unresolved, assigned to "the group" per the Gemini next-steps list): is this genuinely unique to Unisys, or is it just how Agentforce dispatch is generally configured — which would matter for every future Agentforce buyer, not just Unisys.

- **Same-site is explicitly the first, narrow slice of a much bigger "geography + batch" capability.** The group was clear this isn't a standalone feature — solving same-site is meant to unlock belief/trust with Unisys while the team builds toward broader batch-aware, geography-aware dispatch (evaluating a whole day's work across a territory against available capacity, closer to true clustering). That broader work is explicitly not scoped yet.

- **A provider-driven alternative to system bundling was floated but not adopted for now.** Instead of the system or buyer deciding groupings, the alternative discussed was: when a provider requests one work order, proactively surface other nearby open work orders so they can build their own bundle (with gamification floated — "request all three and you're more likely to get them"). The group preferred starting with the simpler same-site/geocoding match and holding this two-sided idea for a later "nearby, not identical" phase, partly because once providers trust that FN's routing is reasonable, exact optimality on nearby (vs. same-site) groupings matters less.

- **Deterministic dispatch reaffirmed over AI/heuristic models, with historical scar tissue as the reason.** The team previously tried letting buyers tweak the provider-ranking algorithm (trading quality for proximity, to reduce cost/time-to-fill) and scrapped it — hard criteria plus the existing quality-team ranking model proved sufficient, and layering buyer-tunable weights on top got complicated fast (e.g., unresolved tie-breaks between a closer-but-lower-quality provider and a farther-but-higher-quality one). A previously-raised compliance concern also resurfaced: potential legislation requiring human intervention in AI-driven dispatch decisions, and the risk of an AI systematically favoring one provider over another. Neither was dug into further since the team never went down that path — noted as a real open risk if AI-driven ranking is revisited later. Conclusion: keep the deterministic core, with AI/agentic pieces potentially sitting *beside* it (e.g., translating buyer language into rules, or handling escalation/outreach) rather than replacing it.

- **Continuous, indefinite dispatch proposed to replace fixed time windows.** Rather than a hard 4- or 8-hour dispatch window, the group discussed having the system keep attempting dispatch indefinitely (or up to a buyer-set outer bound) until either a successful assignment or a clear escalation trigger (e.g., notifying technicians directly, manual outreach) — modeled on DoorDash's approach, which reportedly moved their service-territory coverage from ~10% to ~60% this way. A related mechanic discussed: don't assign off the very first provider request — hold requests for a short window or until a "confidence score" threshold is met, so the system evaluates multiple responses rather than locking in prematurely.

- **Visibility/engagement metrics are acknowledged as weak.** The only current signal is a "view work order" event, which the group agrees doesn't capture whether a provider actually saw the work (e.g., scrolling past it in a list). Discussed adding front-end tracking for scroll-into-view. Separately, Smart Dispatch-published work orders currently look identical to any other published work order in the provider experience (same notification bell/email/list, no dedicated page or distinguishing push, unlike routed work) — seen as a real barrier to getting eyes on Smart Dispatch work quickly. The old "Recommended Work Orders" feature (built years ago under Tony, tied to skills/select-networks filtering) was confirmed dead; repurposing it was floated but not decided.

- **Capacity caps flagged as an urgent, unsolved gap — "fake it till you make it" is the plan.** Field Nation currently has no way to state a provider's or territory's realistic daily throughput, unlike Agentforce's configurable per-crew capacity (e.g., "this crew can absorb 40 hours/day"). Decision: approximate capacity from historical volume until enough real assignment data exists to calculate it properly — an explicitly acknowledged stopgap. Separately flagged, unresolved: max-assignments-per-day is a single flat rule across all providers today, not configurable per provider/tier, which will conflict with same-site clustering logic (e.g., a flat cap of 4 could force breaking up a natural same-site cluster if a provider already holds 3 WOs elsewhere).

- **Possible bot/API abuse surfaced during the outlier analysis feeding the capacity discussion.** While reviewing extreme cases to inform capacity limits, the team found a provider credited with checking in and out of 200 work orders at a single site (a school in Maplewood, MO) in one day (~$8,000 earned), with check-in/check-out gaps of roughly 0–1 seconds — and a separate case of 79 in a day. The team's read is this is more likely scripted/API-token abuse than manual work completion. This is also feeding a related complaint from Unisys, whose ServiceNow instance reportedly can't handle the performance load of that many rapid back-to-back check-ins. Flagged as a data-integrity concern, not yet assigned to anyone to investigate further.

- **AWS's transportation-logistics service floated for future travel-time/routing work, with real skepticism attached.** One participant relayed direct pushback they'd gotten from AWS reps on some of their own marketed capabilities (e.g., claiming real-time technician tracking and traffic-aware routing, while privately admitting they don't know if/how customers actually use those features). Framed as worth an exploratory call (free, low-commitment) rather than an endorsed direction.

- **Agentforce adoption beyond Unisys is expected but not yet confirmed elsewhere.** Closing comment: Unisys is the only confirmed Field Nation buyer currently on Salesforce Agentforce for dispatch, though more buyers are expected to move that direction — reportedly because Agentforce's own pitch leans on deterministic, rules-based translation of buyer intent (AI interpreting language into rules), which echoes the meeting's throughline that buyers want explainability, not open-ended AI decisioning.
