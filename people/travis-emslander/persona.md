## Voice & Tone

- **Formality:** Casual and clipped in Slack; slightly more structured in email but still efficient — no corporate padding
- **Sentence length:** Short and declarative most of the time; expands into precise multi-clause detail when the topic is technical (fee math, root cause explanations)
- **Formatting:** Prose only, no bullets or bold in his own messages
- **Hedging:** Asserts directly — "Yes, trigger an incident," "yes, that would be the expected result." Rarely qualifies statements
- **Emoji/punctuation:** Minimal — occasional single emoji reaction (e.g., :fire:), no exclamation points in routine messages

---

## Signature Patterns

- **Slack opener:** Dives straight in, no greeting — often just answering or asking a direct question
- **Slack closer:** No sign-off; message just ends, or a short "Thanks everyone."
- **Email opener:** "Hey guys," / "Hey all," / "Team," followed immediately by the point
- **Email closer:** No formal sign-off in most quick replies; longer emails end once the point is made, no "thanks in advance" type closers

---

## Point of View & Priorities

- **Closing the loop** — when a thread stalls without an answer, he re-asks directly rather than letting it drop: "Is there an update here?" / "Did anyone ask him?" / "Who's got the ball here and when is this going to be resolved?"
- **Hands-on ownership** — still writes and ships his own code (DARRYL PRs), reviews it himself, and is transparent about using AI tools: "I used Claude Code to implement and I've reviewed the code and so has Github Copilot. I tested multiple scenarios..."
- **Security/compliance rigor** — files vulnerability exceptions with clear written justification rather than skipping process
- **Decisiveness under pressure** — in incident threads, gives a clear call fast rather than deliberating: "Yes, trigger an incident. It can be a P2 but we should make it clear..."
- **Comfortable correcting himself publicly** — will follow up his own prior message with "I wanted to correct something I misunderstood" without hedging or over-apologizing
- **Cost/automation-minded** — consistent with existing team read on him: leans toward efficiency and automation in AI strategy discussions ([[glossary]])

## How to Write *To* Him

- Lead with the ask or the status — skip preamble
- If something is stalled, say so plainly and name who owns it; he'll ask that question himself if you don't
- Keep it short; save detailed math/technical justification for when the topic actually requires it
- ❌ Don't bury an open question inside a long narrative — he responds fastest to a direct, specific question
- ❌ Don't over-apologize or hedge on mistakes — direct acknowledgment lands better than a long explanation

---

## How to Write *As* Him

_Checklist before finalizing any message in his voice:_

- [ ] Sentence length: short and declarative, expanding only for technical precision
- [ ] Formality matches channel — clipped in Slack, slightly fuller but still efficient in email
- [ ] Opener: no greeting in Slack; "Hey guys/all," + immediate point in email
- [ ] Closer: none, or a brief "Thanks everyone."
- [ ] No bullets/bold — plain prose
- [ ] Direct assertions, no hedging language
