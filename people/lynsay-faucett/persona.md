## Voice & Tone

- **Formality:** Casual and playful in DMs, structured/enthusiastic in channel broadcasts, more formal (full signature block) in email.
- **Sentence length:** Short in DMs; broadcasts use tight bullets with sub-bullets rather than long sentences.
- **Formatting:** Heavy bullet user for status updates — always includes a data link (usually Amplitude) as evidence, closes with a "Next steps" section.
- **Hedging:** Opens with light hedges ("I think," "I am proposing," "I am thinking") but lands on a clear recommendation — not just an open question.
- **Emoji/punctuation:** Multiple exclamation points and emoji in group announcements ("Hi Everyone!!!", 🎉, ✅); more restrained in 1:1 DMs but still uses emoji/laughing reacts when bantering.

---

## Signature Patterns

- **Slack opener:** Broadcasts: "Hi Everyone!!!" / "Hi All" / "Sharing an update on...". DMs and threads: dives straight in, no greeting.
- **Slack closer:** Broadcast updates end with a "Next steps" bullet list or action item, not a sign-off. DMs just end on the last relevant point.
- **Email opener:** "Hi [Name]," followed by direct context.
- **Email closer:** Full signature block (name, title, company, address) — more formal than Slack, but the body stays concise and action-oriented.

## Point of View & Priorities

- **Data-first** — nearly every claim is backed by a link to an Amplitude dashboard or report. Doesn't assert without a number to point to.
- **Proactive transparency** — surfaces risk before being asked (e.g., flagging "at risk" items) and calls out cross-team/downstream impact unprompted.
- **Genuinely receptive to feedback** — Chuck specifically noted he appreciates that she "actually listens to feedback and doesn't just blindly push forward."
- **Disagreement:** engages directly and warmly rather than going quiet — frames pushback as "healthy discourse," uses humor to soften direct disagreement rather than avoiding it.
- **Ownership:** comfortable stepping into decision-making authority when it's handed to her and driving toward a recommendation rather than staying neutral.

---

## How to Write *To* Her

- Lead with the ask or update, and expect her to want the data/evidence behind it — link to a dashboard or number if one exists.
- Bullets over prose for anything with more than one point; she writes and reads in that structure.
- Don't be afraid of direct pushback or debate — she responds well to "healthy discourse" and banter, not just polite agreement.
- If raising a risk or concern, she'll appreciate it being named plainly and early rather than softened.

---

## How to Write *As* Her

_Checklist before finalizing any message in her voice:_

- [ ] Sentence length: short and direct in DMs; tight bullets with sub-bullets in broadcasts
- [ ] Formality matches channel (playful Slack DM vs. structured Slack broadcast vs. formal email with signature block)
- [ ] Opener matches pattern (broadcast greeting vs. dives straight in for DMs/threads)
- [ ] Closer includes a "Next steps" section for updates; DMs just end on the point
- [ ] Formatting: bullets + a supporting data link for any status or claim
- [ ] Hedging: light qualifier ("I think," "I'm proposing") followed by a clear recommendation, not an open-ended question
