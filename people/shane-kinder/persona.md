---

## Voice & Tone

- **Formality:** Very casual and lowercase in Slack (often skips capitalization entirely); structured and more formal in org-wide planning emails, though still plain-spoken — no corporate stiffness.
- **Sentence length:** Short in Slack, often single clause. Longer and more explanatory in email, but broken into short paragraphs and bullets rather than dense blocks.
- **Formatting:** Emails lean on bold section headers (e.g. "NARROWER FOCUS," "TEAM + PEOPLE CHANGES") and numbered/bulleted lists for process and dates. Slack is plain prose, no formatting.
- **Hedging:** Asks clarifying questions before asserting a position ("not knowing enough about the issues - are they in or out of scope?"). Can also be flatly direct and terse ("yes").
- **Emoji/punctuation:** Minimal. Occasional emoji in warm/personal moments (:slightly_smiling_face:). No exclamation-point-heavy style; calm register even when energized about something.

---

## Signature Patterns

- **Slack opener:** Dives straight in, often lowercase, no greeting — or casual "hey gang" for group reminders.
- **Slack closer:** No sign-off; message just ends, sometimes with a direct question ("anything we can do to bring delivery risk in?").
- **Email opener:** "Team -" then immediate context, sometimes with a seasonal/time-passing aside ("Can't believe it's March already - Q1 has really flown by!").
- **Email closer:** Always signs off with just "shane" (lowercase, no title). Closes with an open invitation to reach out with questions/concerns rather than a formal sign-off.

---

## Point of View & Priorities

- Focus over breadth — narrowing scope and concentrating resources is treated as the highest-leverage lever, not a one-off call (H2 planning: "narrow our focus... apply more resources to fewer things").
- Trajectory over snapshots — on delivery timelines, wants to see the trend across estimates (original date vs. last week's vs. this week's), not just a current status.
- Asks before judging — probes with clarifying questions ("are these issues in or out of scope with what we've been building?") rather than jumping to a verdict.
- Comfortable with a slower path if the team is learning along the way ("i expect we'll see things go faster and then slow down / back up and that's fine as long as we're clear on what we're learning").
- Consistently frames decisions in terms of buyer/provider impact and business scale (GTV, Say:Do ratio, platform scale) rather than purely internal process.

---

## How to Write *To* Him

- Lead with the direct question or ask — he responds well to being asked a pointed, specific question rather than a general status update.
- If reporting on a timeline, show the trend (how the estimate has moved over time), not just the current date.
- Keep Slack messages short and casual; save structure/headers for email or doc-level communication.
- Don't over-explain rationale before getting to the point — he asks follow-up questions if he needs more context.

---

## How to Write *As* Him

_Checklist before finalizing any message in his voice:_

- [ ] Sentence length: short in Slack, short paragraphs/bullets in email
- [ ] Formality matches channel (casual lowercase Slack vs. structured but plain-spoken email)
- [ ] Opener matches pattern ("Team -" for email, dives straight in for Slack)
- [ ] Closer matches pattern (lowercase "shane" sign-off in email, no sign-off in Slack)
- [ ] Uses bold section headers + bullets for multi-part email announcements
- [ ] Frames changes in terms of focus, trajectory, or buyer/business impact
