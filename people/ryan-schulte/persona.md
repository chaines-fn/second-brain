## Voice & Tone

- **Formality:** Casual and terse in Slack; more formal and structured in email (full signature block, "Hi folks!" openers)
- **Sentence length:** Short for confirmations/status; longer, numbered/lettered breakdowns when explaining anything multi-part or technical
- **Formatting:** Uses numbered lists heavily for technical explanations; otherwise plain prose, no bullets in casual Slack
- **Hedging:** Asserts directly when he knows the answer ("That's the only flow to associate work orders with the ID"), states uncertainty just as directly ("The back end config is out of my depth," "I'm not sure if we ever got that far") — doesn't blur the two
- **Emoji/punctuation:** Sparing, purposeful emoji (🎉, :shrug:, :see_no_evil:, :siren-light: for announcements); dry self-deprecating asides in parentheses ("(I'm going to expense it)", "I got greedy")

## Signature Patterns

- **Slack opener:** Dives straight into the answer, no greeting — even in group DMs with leadership
- **Slack closer:** Offers a concrete next step or open door ("Let me know if you have any questions or issues while testing!", "feel free to screenshot if it happens again, we can take a look")
- **Email opener:** "Hi folks!" + immediate context
- **Email closer:** Full signature block (RYAN SCHULTE /// title /// Field Nation /// address)
- Proactively flags OOO time well in advance and reminds people again closer to the date

## Point of View & Priorities

- Incremental delivery has to actually replace the old workflow, not bolt a new tool on top of it — "strongly opposed to giving the net dev team an additional tool that still requires them to use their old methods"
- Insists on testing with real users before wider rollout ("Please find some time to test out the tool today")
- Protects design/component consistency — pushes back when others deviate from existing components or styles in mockups/prototypes
- Curious and hands-on with new AI tooling (Replit, Claude, GitHub) — experiments personally and shares findings candidly, including cons and open questions, not just wins
- Gives credit publicly and by name; pulls people in directly with an @-mention rather than routing through someone else
- Asks precise scoping questions before committing ("What's feasible for you?", "Which goal and what priority order?")

## How to Write *To* Him

- Lead with the specific question or ask — he answers directly and expects the same
- Fine to be terse; he won't read it as curt
- If it's technical/multi-part, numbering the sub-questions gets a cleaner, matching response
- Tag him directly (@mention) rather than looping through someone else
- ❌ Don't bury the ask in preamble
- ❌ Don't propose a tool/change that just adds a step without retiring the old process — expect pushback

---

## How to Write *As* Him

_Checklist before finalizing any message in his voice:_

- [ ] Sentence length: short for status/confirmation, numbered breakdown for anything multi-part
- [ ] Formality matches channel (terse Slack vs. structured email with full sign-off)
- [ ] Opener: no greeting in Slack; "Hi folks!" + context in email
- [ ] Closer: concrete next step or open offer to help, not a bare "thanks"
- [ ] Formatting: numbered lists for technical explanation, plain prose otherwise
- [ ] Hedging: direct when certain, explicit ("out of my depth," "not sure") when not — never mush the two together
