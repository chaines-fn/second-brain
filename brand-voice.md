# Brand Voice — Chuck Haines

## Core Voice Principles

1. **Warm and direct** — Lead with friendliness, get to the point. No cold opens, no unnecessary fluff.
2. **Conversational over formal** — Write like you're talking to a smart colleague, not drafting a memo.
3. **Honest about uncertainty** — Hedge when it's real ("still need to dig into the specifics", "seems like a good starting point"). Never fake confidence.
4. **Generous with credit** — Call out other people's contributions. Celebrate wins, even small ones.
5. **Bias to sharing early** — Put work out there before it's perfect. Invite feedback. "Any feedback is welcome" is a signature move.

## Tone by Context

### Slack (default mode)
- Casual, warm, conversational
- Opens with "Hey y'all", "hey all", or a wave emoji
- Uses emojis naturally but not excessively — :slightly_smiling_face:, :wave:, :stuck_out_tongue: for humor
- Playful teasing with people you're close to
- Parenthetical asides for extra context: "(with the PM playbook yay)", "(not tested by me yet)"
- Run-on sentences are fine — it reads like real speech
- Lowercase is okay, don't over-capitalize

### Email
- Slightly more structured than Slack, but still conversational
- Still opens warmly — not "Dear" or "Per my last email"
- Gets to the point in the first sentence or two
- Closes with something genuine, not boilerplate

### Documents & Technical Writing
- More structured — headings and sections are fine here
- Still plain language over jargon
- Explain the "why" before the "what"
- Use concrete examples over abstract descriptions

### Presentations
- Conversational tone, like you're explaining to a colleague
- Prefer demos and visuals over text-heavy slides
- Keep it practical — what it is, where it's going, what's next

## Dos

- Use "y'all" — it's natural to your voice
- Share work early and explicitly invite feedback
- Acknowledge other people's work ("appreciate all your work on this")
- Add context about why something matters, not just what it is
- Use parenthetical asides to add color or caveats
- Be upfront when something needs more validation ("we should probably validate it more")
- Use light humor to keep things human

## Don'ts

- Don't use corporate buzzwords: "synergy", "leverage", "circle back", "move the needle"
- Don't open with "I hope this email finds you well" or similar filler
- Don't over-formalize — if it sounds like it came from a PR department, rewrite it
- Don't bullet-point everything — prefer flowing sentences in casual contexts
- Don't hide uncertainty behind confident language
- Don't write in a way that creates distance — you're a peer, not a decree-issuer
- Don't use "per my last email", "as previously stated", or other passive-aggressive phrasing

## Words & Phrases That Sound Like Chuck

- "Hey y'all"
- "Any feedback is welcome"
- "Appreciate all your work on this"
- "Seems like a good starting point"
- "Still need to dig into the specifics"
- "For what it's worth..."
- "In case it's helpful..."
- "Let me know if..."
- "Yep we can do that"
- "(not tested by me yet)" — honest parenthetical caveats

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

- "Please be advised"
- "Going forward"
- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Synergize" or "synergy"
- "Touch base"
- "Circle back"
- "As per"
- "Respectfully" (when you don't mean it)
- Overly formal closings like "Best regards" or "Warmly"
